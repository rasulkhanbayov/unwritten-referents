#######################################################################
# READ THIS BEFORE YOU RUN ANYTHING. THESE TWO THINGS DECIDE THE PAPER.
#######################################################################

  (1) RUN E0 FIRST AND TREAT IT AS A STOP GATE.

      E0 checks that the frozen gate directions reproduce the known
      Ferrando et al. (2025) known/unknown entity separation on ONE-HOP
      prompts. If they do not, your measuring instrument is broken and
      every result after it is uninterpretable. A null result in E1 would
      then mean "my probe does not work", not "the model is blind".
      Those two are indistinguishable from the E1 output alone, which is
      why E0 has to come first.

      Do not proceed past E0 until at least two of the three gate
      estimators clear the threshold on HELD-OUT entities. If E0 fails,
      flag back (see FLAG-BACK TRIGGERS) rather than tuning until it
      passes. Tuning the gate on the data you will later test on
      destroys the result.

  (2) CONDITION EVERY PRIMARY ANALYSIS ON K1 = 1.

      A two-hop query the model gets wrong because it failed the FIRST
      hop tells you nothing about self-knowledge composition. If you do
      not filter these out, you will measure first-hop failure and
      report it as a blind spot. Reviewers will find this immediately
      and it is fatal.

      Filter on BOTH:
        (a) K1 = 1, the model answers the first hop correctly when asked
            in isolation; and
        (b) independent evidence the bridge was resolved internally,
            obtained by decoding the bridge position (Patchscopes,
            following Biran et al. 2024).

      Report the retained fraction. Repeat unrestricted in the appendix.

  One more, because the code caught me making this mistake:
  NEVER call estimate_shortcut_v() without passing features. The
  marginal shortcut rate under-estimates the essential supremum and
  makes the Proposition 2 floor ANTI-CONSERVATIVE, which fabricates an
  apparent violation of a theorem that actually holds. The released code
  now refuses the marginal by default. Use floor_report() and you cannot
  get this wrong.

#######################################################################
# 1. CHEAP PILOT. DO THIS FIRST, IT TAKES AN AFTERNOON.
#######################################################################

  Purpose: find out whether the whole idea is alive before you spend
  weeks on it.

  Scope:  Gemma-2-2B only. 300 two-hop queries. One SAE layer, the one
          Ferrando et al. report as the peak for entity latents.

  Steps:
    P1. Label K1, K2, Y on the 300 queries by alias-aware exact match.
    P2. Report the joint distribution of (K1, K2, Y). You need a
        reasonable number of cases with K1=1 and K2=0. If that cell is
        nearly empty, your query sample is too easy; resample toward
        less popular bridge entities and repeat.
    P3. Extract the gate at the surface entity position. Compute
        AUC(S -> K1) and AUC(S -> K2).
    P4. Run floor_report() from skc_metrics.

  What you are looking for:
    ALIVE     AUC(S -> K1) clearly above chance AND
              AUC(S -> K2) near chance.
              That is the H1 signature. Proceed to the full protocol.
    DEAD      AUC(S -> K1) also near chance.
              Your gate is not working. Go back to E0, do not proceed.
    INVERTED  AUC(S -> K2) clearly above chance.
              H1 is false and the paper's central hypothesis is wrong.
              This is a real outcome, not a failure. FLAG BACK
              IMMEDIATELY. The honest paper becomes a negative result
              that corrects a plausible structural prediction, and it
              still contributes the floor, the benchmark, and the
              diagnostics. Do not try to rescue H1.

  The pilot is not a result. Do not put pilot numbers in the paper.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------
E0  POSITIVE CONTROL. THE STOP GATE.
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Objective   Confirm the measuring instrument works before using it.
Claim       None directly. E0 makes E1-E4 interpretable.
Reduces     The single most likely reviewer objection to a null result:
            "your probe just does not work."

Data        One-hop prompts only, over the four Ferrando et al. entity
            types plus the TwoHopFact one-hop counterparts.
            Split entities into fit / held-out. Never reuse.

Method      Fit THREE gate estimators on the FIT split only, then FREEZE:
              (i)   SAE latent selection by separation score
                    (Ferrando et al. protocol; separation_score() in
                    skc_metrics.py)
              (ii)  difference-of-means between known and unknown
                    entity activations
              (iii) L2-regularized logistic probe

            "Frozen" means: after this point you never refit on
            anything. A direction refit on two-hop data would only show
            that some linear readout exists. A FROZEN direction that
            transfers to the bridge position shows the bridge state is
            expressed in the format the refusal mechanism already reads.
            That distinction is the whole argument of Section 6.4.
            Do not lose it by refitting for convenience.

Metrics     AUROC per estimator, per model, per entity type, held-out.
            Also AUC(gate -> K2) when the bridge is NAMED explicitly.

Expected    Reproduction of the published separation, and above-chance
            prediction of K2 when the entity is written. The second part
            is the premise whose ABSENCE the rest of the paper tests.

GATE        Proceed only if >= 2 of 3 estimators clear threshold on
            held-out entities. Record the threshold BEFORE running.

E1  THE BLIND SPOT AND ITS SILENCE.  (main result)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Objective   Decide between H1, H2, H3, and quantify the decomposition.

THE HEADLINE IS PART (d), NOT PART (a). Everyone reports AUROCs.
The memorable result is that the model's own abstention signal is
JUST AS CONFIDENT on composed queries as on single-hop queries it
answers correctly, while being far more often wrong. That is what
makes the failure silent rather than merely present. Compute the
two-sample distance between the gate's score distributions and the
accuracy gap at matched gate score. Lead with it.

ALSO REQUIRED: run blind_spot_decomposition() at several coverages.
A large blind term with a small subopt term means the shortfall is
in WHERE the rule looks, so no better scoring function on the same
positions can fix it. The reverse pattern means our framing is wrong
and the problem is ordinary estimator quality. Report both.

NOTE ON SIGNS: non-negativity of the two terms is a POPULATION
statement. Cross-fitted plug-in estimates can go slightly negative;
on synthetic data the violations sit near a tenth of a binomial
standard error. The code tolerates violations inside sampling noise
and RAISES on anything larger. If it raises, you have an estimation
bug (eta not out-of-fold, reference not a superset, folds too small),
not a discovery.
Claim       The surface gate sees hop 1 and not hop 2.
Reduces     The risk that the effect is a granularity artifact.

Data        Two-hop queries, RESTRICTED to K1=1 with verified bridge
            resolution. Report the retained fraction.

Method      Read the frozen gate at the surface entity position.
            Compute AUC against Y, K1, K2.
            Run floor_report() for delta, v, floor, and the
            confidence-based violation check.
            Plot the risk-coverage curve against the floor.

Baselines   Named-bridge control: the same query with the descriptive
            mention replaced by the bridge name. The gap MUST close
            here. If it does not, something other than latency is
            driving your effect.

Metrics     AUC(S->K1), AUC(S->K2), AUC(S->Y), delta-hat, v-hat,
            floor, AURC. Percentile bootstrap, 2000 resamples.

Expected    Under H1: AUC(S->K1) high, AUC(S->K2) near chance,
            delta near zero, and the risk-coverage curve flattening onto
            the floor instead of descending.

WRITE IT EITHER WAY. A large delta-hat refutes the paper's hypothesis
and you report that.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------
E2  CAUSAL TESTS.  (Table 1 design)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Objective   Separate "no signal" from "signal not routed".
Claim       The bridge is causally used for recall and not for refusal.
Reduces     "This is just correlational probing."

C1 Surface steer
   Add the unknown-entity direction at the surface entity position.
   Select the steering coefficient on a VALIDATION split, as in
   Ferrando et al. Do not tune it on your test set.
   Measure refusal rate split by K2. H1 predicts refusal rises
   regardless of K2, that is, the intervention is not selective.

C2 Bridge patch  <-- THE DECISIVE ONE
   Replace the bridge-position residual with that of a matched query
   whose bridge is unknown. Match on: relation type, prompt length,
   surface entity popularity decile.
   Measure, ON THE SAME QUERIES AND THE SAME PATCH:
     - change in target-token recall
     - change in refusal rate
   Report the dissociation as the ratio of STANDARDIZED effects with a
   paired bootstrap interval. Unstandardized effects are not comparable
   across two differently scaled outcomes; do not skip the
   standardization.

C4 Graph trace  <-- ADDED AFTER THE 2026 LITERATURE PASS
   Compute attribution graphs with circuit-tracer (Hanna et al. 2025)
   on matched composed and named-bridge prompts. Ask whether a path
   exists from bridge-position knowledge features to the refusal
   features, and compare its summed edge weight against the path from
   the surface entity.

   This makes "not routed" a DIRECTLY MEASURED property of the
   computational graph rather than an inference from behavior. The
   field moved to transcoders and attribution graphs during 2025-2026;
   a 2027 reviewer will ask why you did not use the tool built for
   exactly this feature-to-feature question.

   circuit-tracer supports Gemma-2-2B (Gemma Scope transcoders),
   Llama-3.1-1B and Qwen3-4B. Neuronpedia offers on-demand graph
   generation for Gemma-2-2B. Budget: graphs are large (hundreds to
   thousands of nodes) and annotation is slow.

   CAUTION: attribution graphs describe a REPLACEMENT model, not the
   model itself. Following Lindsey et al. (2025), validate every
   graph-derived claim by intervening in the underlying model. Do not
   report a graph property as a fact about the model without that step.

   Use the named-bridge prompts as the positive control: there the
   entity IS written, so a path SHOULD appear. If it does not, you have
   a graph-construction problem, not a finding.

C3 Gate inject
   Add the unknown-entity direction at the BRIDGE position.
   Little effect  -> the routing pathway is absent; a repair must add
                     routing.
   Refusal rises  -> the pathway exists but is not driven by the real
                     bridge knowledge state; a repair only needs to
                     supply the signal.
   Either outcome is publishable and they imply different repairs.

E3  THE SIGNAL IS PRESENT AND UNUSED.  (Section 6.4)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Objective   Show the bridge state is decodable by the FROZEN direction.
Claim       The failure is routing, not information.
Reduces     "The model obviously cannot gate on what it never computed."
            This experiment is what makes the paper non-trivial.

Method      Take the frozen surface-entity gate direction from E0.
            Evaluate it at the bridge-resolution position. DO NOT REFIT.
            Sweep layers x positions and report the FULL map, not the
            maximum. Reporting only the argmax over a large sweep is
            selection on noise.
            Permutation null over positions to calibrate the maximum.

Metrics     AUROC against K2 at each (layer, position). Peak layer.
            Permutation null distribution.

Expected    Above-chance transfer near the bridge-resolution layer
            identified by the Biran et al. protocol, and chance
            elsewhere.

E4  REPAIR.  (Table 3)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Objective   Show the floor is escapable.
Claim       Routing the latent gate recovers rho of the blind spot.

Methods     BGR-min: s = min(z(g_S), z(g_B)). No trained parameters.
            BGR-lr:  cross-fitted two-feature logistic regression.

Baselines   COMPARE WITHIN INFERENCE-COST CLASSES. This is how the
            hallucination-detection literature compares methods, and it
            is the only fair framing: SEP's own paper notes it "approaches
            but does not match 10x costlier baselines", and ICR Probe
            flags its comparison with Semantic Entropy as
            "computationally imbalanced". Reporting a single leaderboard
            across cost classes is a known way to look better than you
            are, and reviewers in this area know it.

            Class 0 - one forward pass, no extra generation:
              surface gate (Ferrando et al. 2025)
              accuracy probe / final-token probe (Tamoyan-style)
              Semantic Entropy Probes (Kossen et al. 2024)
              HaloScope (Du et al. 2024)         <-- widely used as the
                                                     "highest performing
                                                     open baseline"
              INSIDE (Chen et al. 2024)
              ICR Probe (2507.16488)
              *** BGR-min and BGR-lr live HERE ***
            Class 1 - one extra call:
              P(True) (Kadavath et al. 2022), verbalized confidence
            Class 2 - k generations:
              Semantic Entropy (Farquhar et al., Nature 2024)
              SelfCheckGPT (Manakul et al. 2023)
              self-consistency, k=10
            Class 3 - decomposition pipelines (multi-call):
              Self-Ask (Press et al. 2023)
              Decomposed-prompting abstention (Madhwal et al. 2026)
              Operadic consistency (2606.13649)

            CLASS 3 IS THE ONE THAT CAN KILL THE PAPER'S PRACTICAL CLAIM.
            "Why do I need a bridge-gate readout when I can just prompt
            the model to decompose the question?" is the obvious
            objection and it now has published methods behind it. You
            must run these. Two outcomes, both fine:
              - BGR matches them at a fraction of the cost -> the cost
                argument carries the contribution.
              - They beat BGR -> report it. They work by making the
                latent entity explicit, which is our mechanism by a more
                expensive route, so it CONFIRMS the account even while
                beating the method. Say exactly that. Do not hide it.

            Report for EVERY selector: forward passes, generated tokens,
            wall-clock per query. A method that wins by spending 10x has
            not won in the class that matters.

            >>> TO RUN: re-search for stronger baselines shortly before
            submission. This list was current as of August 2026.

Metrics     AUROC, AURC, risk at 50% coverage, Delta_blind, rho, ECE
            (Guo et al. 2017), and the Abstain-QA / AUCM protocol.
            AUROC alone is not enough for an abstention paper in 2026.
            Paired bootstrap and DeLong for every comparison.

            USE recovery_fraction(), NOT recoverable_fraction_rho().
            The paper's rho is the share of Delta_blind that the repair
            closes, at a stated coverage. The old essential-supremum rho
            was RETIRED and is kept in the code only as an estimator
            sanity check. Mixing the two in one table is a real error.

            Do NOT pass a repair (which reads the ENLARGED set) into
            blind_spot_decomposition(). Lemma 1's guarantee that
            Delta_subopt >= 0 holds only for a rule reading the SMALLER
            set, so the guardrail will fire, correctly. This bug was
            caught by the smoke test during development.

Expected    BGR beats the surface gate. If BGR-min matches BGR-lr, the
            effect is structural rather than learned, which is the
            STRONGER outcome. Say so if it happens.

E5  COMPOUNDING ACROSS HOPS  (Proposition 4)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Objective   Show the blind spot grows with chain length.
Claim       The uncovered fraction rises with k, so agentic pipelines are
            worse off than two-hop QA.

Data        Family F2, three-hop Wikidata chains, with all decomposed
            one-hop prompts so K_2 and K_3 are both labelable.

            USE NESTED CHAINS. Compare each three-hop chain against ITS
            OWN two-hop prefix, not against a separate two-hop set. The
            monotonicity clause of Theorem 1 is a POINTWISE statement,
            M_{k+1} = M_k * K_{k+1} <= M_k, which only makes sense when
            the two indicators live on the same items. Across unrelated
            query sets they are defined on different probability spaces
            and the theorem says nothing, so a rise you observe there
            could be a dataset difference rather than compounding.

Method      khop_floor() in skc_metrics.py. Compute P(M_k=0), delta_k,
            v_k and the floor for k=2 and k=3 on matched item sets.

CAUTION     Proposition 4 bounds the LEADING term, P(M_k=0), which is
            provably non-decreasing in k. It does NOT prove the floor
            itself rises, because delta_k and v_k could grow faster.
            That is exactly why this is an experiment. If the slack terms
            grow faster and the floor falls, report that; it is
            informative and does not contradict the proposition.

Expected    P(M_k=0) rises with k. Whether the floor rises is open.

E6  CHAIN OF THOUGHT: DOES WRITING THE ENTITY RESTORE GATING?
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
Objective   Test our mechanism against a competing published finding.
Claim       CoT helps factual reliability partly by converting latent
            entities into written ones, which restores the gate.

Why it      Cheng et al. (Findings EMNLP 2025) report that CoT OBSCURES
matters     hallucination cues. Our account predicts the gate at the
            self-written mention becomes MORE informative. These are
            separable on the same items: theirs concerns aggregate
            detection scores, ours concerns the entity gate at a specific
            position. Measure both. A head-to-head test against a
            published prediction is worth far more than an ablation.

Data        Family F3: F1 items, answered with chain-of-thought prompting
            on instruction-tuned and reasoning-tuned models.

Method      Locate the model's own written mention of the bridge entity
            in its trace. Read the FROZEN gate there. Compare against the
            same items answered directly.

            Watch out: the model may write a WRONG bridge entity. Split
            the analysis by whether the written bridge is correct. Gating
            on a confidently-wrong bridge is a different and also
            interesting result; do not pool the two.

Metrics     Gate AUROC against K_2 at the self-written mention; abstention
            quality; the Cheng et al. detection metrics on the same items.

Expected    If restored: a mechanism-level account of part of what CoT
            does, and a cheap mitigation for agentic pipelines.
            If not restored: our account of CoT is wrong. Say so plainly.

E7  ABLATIONS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
  (a) Named-bridge control (gap must close)
  (b) Popularity-matched subsets, by Wikipedia pageview decile.
      This is not optional. Without it a reviewer will say you measured
      entity frequency, not knowledge.
  (c) Length-matched distractor control
  (d) All three gate estimators agree
  (e) Three-hop queries: does the gap compound?
  (f) Instruction-tuned versus base
  (h) Attention control: confirm the bridge position RECEIVES substantial
      attention. If it does not, "the gate is not routed" collapses into
      "the position is ignored", which is a weaker and different claim.
      This control is what licenses the routing interpretation.

  --- ADDED BY THE EXPERIMENTS AUDIT. These are not optional. ---

  (i) COUNTERFACTUAL CHAINS. Wikipedia/Wikidata multi-hop benchmarks are
      documented to over-state composition because models memorize the
      composite; counterfactual and knowledge-edited variants recover a
      15-25 point gap. Our shortcut term v measures exactly this, so
      report v AND rerun the headline result on counterfactual chains
      whose composites cannot have been memorized. Without this a
      reviewer can attribute the whole effect to memorization.

  (j) RANDOM-DIRECTION NEGATIVE CONTROL. A direction of matched norm
      drawn at random must produce no effect in C1-C3. Without this,
      every intervention result is uninterpretable, because any
      large enough perturbation changes behavior.

  (k) LABEL VALIDATION. Alias-aware exact match is reproducible but
      under-counts unusual surface forms. Validate against an LLM judge
      (Zheng et al. 2023) on a random subsample; report agreement. Keep
      exact match primary. If any headline result flips depending on
      which labeler you use, report that fact rather than picking one.

  (l) CROSS-TYPE GENERALIZATION. Fit the gate on one entity type and the
      bridge readout on one relation type, then test on held-out types.
      Without this the readout may be a relation-specific artifact.

  (m) TEMPLATE AND PARAPHRASE ROBUSTNESS. Tamoyan et al. found some
      templates introduce spurious correlations and that only one of
      their four survived. Screen yours the same way.

  (n) REASONING-TUNED MODELS. Kirichenko et al. (2025) report that
      reasoning fine-tuning DEGRADES abstention and that scale barely
      matters. That competes directly with the E6 prediction. Run at
      least one reasoning-tuned model and report both outcomes.

  (o) FAITHFULNESS. For C4, report the FRACTION of graph-derived claims
      that survive intervention in the underlying model, not just the
      ones that survived. Attribution graphs describe a replacement
      model; an unvalidated graph property is not a fact about the model.

  (p) POWER. State the minimum detectable effect your item counts
      support, computed BEFORE collection, for the primary comparison.


#######################################################################
# 3. FLAG-BACK TRIGGERS. STOP AND DISCUSS, DO NOT IMPROVISE.
#######################################################################

  - E0 fails on more than one estimator.
  - AUC(S -> K2) is clearly ABOVE chance in the pilot or E1.
    H1 is false. This is a real finding. Do not rescue the hypothesis.
  - floor_report() reports violated=True with a correctly computed
    conditional v. Either the estimator has a bug or an assumption is
    broken. Investigate before reporting; do not adjust the floor to fit.
  - The K1=1, K2=0 cell is too small to analyze.
  - Effects appear only at one SAE width or one layer.
  - recoverable_fraction_rho() raises on kappa ordering. That is an
    estimation bug signature, not a real effect.
  - A baseline beats BGR. Report it. Do not drop the baseline.
  - blind_spot_decomposition() raises. That is an estimation fault,
    not a finding. Fix it before reporting anything from that run.
  - The blind term is SMALL and the subopt term is large. Our framing
    is wrong: the problem is estimator quality, not read position.
    Flag back before writing.
  - The bridge position turns out to receive little attention (E7h). The
    routing interpretation weakens; reframe before writing.
  - The k=3 floor FALLS because slack grows faster. Not a contradiction
    of Proposition 4. Report it as measured.
  - Anything that would require refitting a "frozen" direction.
